## Summary: the most important findings, most severe first

1. **(error) What CAPRI does when a bound binds is not "solve the square system".** In CAPRI a binding bound
   makes the square system infeasible. CONOPT then returns a least-infeasible point. CAPRI widens the bounds of
   the variables flagged at a bound and re-solves, at most twice (simu_market.gms l. 95-145, widen_bounds.gms). In
   its last iteration it accepts a sum of infeasibilities up to 0.1 as "feasible" (l. 289-300). IPOPT with
   ½‖z−z₀‖² and the same bounds would not return CAPRI's point: the objective plays no role there (see 2). The
   design does not specify this case at all (Q2).
2. **(risk) The objective is practically inert.** CasADi's IPOPT (3.14.19) handles square problems specially: the
   library's own option texts say expect_infeasible_problem is switched on, the run ends when restoration finds a
   feasible point, and the multipliers are recomputed. As long as the system is square, ½‖z−z₀‖² neither selects a
   root nor regularises one. It does matter if G3 is accidentally non-square, and then it hides the error. The
   "price unknowns z" are also undefined in the full-variable form (Q2).
3. **(error) The inventory overstates the TRQ port.**
   - TRQImports_ is marked "ported", but global (erga omnes) quotas are not represented. ARMINGTON_MARKET.md
     l. 145 records them as a registered omission, yet they are missing from the departures register.
   - tarSpec_ is marked "ported", but only its "bilateral TRQ, no global TRQ" branch exists in capri-mod.
   - tarAdval_ is marked "to check", but its ad valorem out-of-quota tariff is frozen in EUR/t at base FOB prices
     (build_bilateral_market.py l. 230-236).
   - Quotas are summed over the CAPRI exporters inside a model exporter, and one sigmoid acts on the sum.
   - Only the EU's quotas are read (l. 212).

   (Q3, Q4)
4. **(error) Two parts of capri-mod's market have no counterpart in m_globMarket, and the register has neither.**
   - The "pooled" commodities, with a world balance (bilateral_solve.py l. 204-228, 524-530). In CAPRI, every
     market product XXX is Armington-traded, and MILK is removed from XXX for the full solve
     (simu_market.gms l. 63). wldMarket_ and nTrd_, which the design maps them to, belong to m_netTrade, not
     m_globMarket (market_model.gms l. 4218-4239).
   - The prices of regions without supply, which are held at their current ratio (trade_ad.py l. 41). CAPRI has
     PMrk_ for these regions (l. 3074-3080).
5. **(error) The inventory's positivity finding is wrong for human consumption.** consFudge_ is dormant in CAPRI's
   simulation as far as I could trace it:
   - set_start_val.gms l. 173 gives v_consQuant a lower bound > 0;
   - widen_bounds.gms l. 88-92 keeps it ≥ 1e-12;
   - the v_consQuantNeg widening is commented out (l. 94-115).

   CAPRI's operative rule is to fix consumption at its level once it reaches ~0 with a nonzero marginal
   (l. 121-123). The fudge equations for production, processing and feed are activated only after widen_bounds
   has pushed a lower bound below zero (l. 48-62; feed l. 192-196). "Take CAPRI's smooth forms in G3"
   is therefore not "as CAPRI" by default: it is a choice to be registered (Q1, Q4).
6. **(gap) The G3 acceptance test cannot detect departures from CAPRI.** "Equal to the current solver within
   market.tol" compares capri-mod with itself. It also conflicts with G3's own aim, because every change toward
   CAPRI's form (consFudge_, lifted lags, removed floors) must break that equality. A replication test on CAPRI's
   own market state is needed (Q5).
7. **(risk) The blocks still contain many kinks the design does not list.** It lists only G2c's two fmax. Others:
   trade_ad.py l. 52 and l. 163, demand.py l. 298, 393 and 482, supply.py l. 741 and 825, and processing's
   max(0, ·) in demand.py l. 393. IPOPT's convergence theory assumes C² functions (Q3, Q6).
8. **(risk) Validation tolerances do not match.** market.tol is 1e-7 (defaults.toml l. 269). CAPRI's final solve
   uses CONOPT feasibility tolerances rtnwmi 5e-6 and rtnwma 5e-4 on equations scaled by base data (conopt.603,
   selected at simu_market.gms l. 88; TOLINFEAS 1e-5, market_model.gms l. 4388). Agreement with CAPRI tighter
   than about 1e-5 in scaled residual is not meaningful (Q5).
9. **(note) The functional forms of the three built blocks are faithful where they are ported.** I verified
   these against the GAMS:
   - Armington CES nests and calibration;
   - the GL demand (G, G_i, F and the symmetrised B);
   - the additive consumer price;
   - ProcMargO_ and ProcNQ_;
   - procYield_: the closed form of its implicit mass correction is exact;
   - ProcO_ and ProcFudge_'s softplus.

   The departures are in the domains, activation, aggregation and floors (Q3).

## Q1. Equivalence of a reduced formulation and CAPRI's full-variable square system

Let CAPRI's system be F(x, y) = 0, with y the quantities defined explicitly by y_k − f_k(x, y_<k) = 0. Then
substituting y = f(x) into F gives a reduced system R(x) = F(x, f(x)) = 0. Its solutions are in bijection with
those of the full system if and only if all of the following hold.

- (a) Each eliminated equation is solved uniquely for its paired variable on the domain considered: explicit, or
  uniquely solvable when implicit.
- (b) No bound on an eliminated variable is active at the solution. In the reduced form the bound becomes a
  nonlinear inequality lo ≤ f(x) ≤ up, or it is dropped.
- (c) The active equation set (GAMS $-domains), the fixed-variable set (HOLDFIXED) and the activation of the "Neg"
  twin variables are identical.
- (d) The same smooth approximations with the same smoothing constants are used.
- (e) The aggregation of regions and products is the same.

Where CAPRI's equations make (b)–(e) bite:

**Q1.1 (risk) The $-domains depend on bounds and levels, so the structure is state-dependent.** Many domains
test `.lo ne .up`, `.range` or `.lo LE 0`. Examples:
- GLDemandGiS_ and XiS_ (l. 860, 891);
- consFudge_, FeedFudge_ and ProcFudge_ (l. 905, 1054, 1158);
- ProdFudge_ (l. 2420) and ProdPriceFudge_ (l. 2380);
- PMrk_ (l. 3074, `NOT v_prodQuant.UP`).

arm2PriceAgg_ even tests the *starting levels* of the import prices (`v_impPrice.l`, l. 3008).

HOLDFIXED = 1 (l. 4381; simu_prestep.gms l. 32-37) turns every fixed variable into a constant. CAPRI keeps the
system square by dropping an equation whenever its variable is fixed, and it aborts if the system is not square
(check_square.gms l. 22, 261-263). A reduced form, and also G3's full form, must reproduce *the same* fixed set and
active set. Examples of what CAPRI fixes:
- v_consQuant for goods without p_pbGL or with HCon ≤ 0.1 (set_start_val.gms l. 176-177; simu_market.gms l. 189);
- v_arm1Price where there is no Armington quantity (set_start_val.gms l. 756);
- v_prodPrice INPE (l. 582);
- v_prodQuant of RMSSUP products without a Hessian (l. 124-126).

*Recommendation:* G3 should build its equation and variable lists from CAPRI's domain rules, evaluated on the
current bounds and levels, and run a check_square equivalent (counts plus a structural-rank or maximum-matching
test) before every solve. Abort as CAPRI does.

**Q1.2 (error in the design's claim; risk) Activation of the fudge equations.** ProdFudge_, ProcFudge_, FeedFudge_,
consFudge_ and tradeFlowFudge_ exist only where the quantity's lower bound is ≤ 0. In that case the NQ equation
defines the free twin `v_*Neg`, and the quantity becomes softplus(v_Neg) = −ncpcm(−v_Neg, 0, z) + 1e-11 (verified,
l. 1158-1163, 2420-2432). The lower bounds are positive at the start:
- procQuant: Proc × 1e-5 (set_start_val.gms l. 34);
- prodQuant: ≥ 1e-8 (l. 101);
- feedQuant: ≥ 1e-10 (l. 181);
- consQuant: HCon × 1e-6 (l. 173).

widen_bounds lowers them below zero only after a solve in which the variable sat at its bound with a nonzero
marginal (widen_bounds.gms l. 48-62 production; l. 125-130 processing; feed l. 192-196). So in CAPRI:
- *x = NQ(p)* when the fudge is inactive (the default);
- *x = softplus(NQ(p))* when it is active, which is history-dependent.

At NQ(p) = 0 the two differ by z·ln 2, with z = 0.01·min(1000, base), i.e. up to about 6.9 kt per cell.

For consumption, the widening never makes the bound ≤ 0: max(1e-12, …), l. 92; the consQuantNeg widening is
commented out, l. 94-115. Consumption is instead *fixed* at its level once it is ≤ 1e-12 with a nonzero marginal
(l. 121-123). Inference: consFudge_ does not enter CAPRI's default simulation. A caveat: I did not trace
load_Market.gms or prep_market's export of bounds, except prep_market.gms l. 850.

The design's claim that substitution "changes no solution" (MARKET_IPOPT_DESIGN.md §3.1) is therefore true only
for a fixed activation state.

*Recommendation:* either reproduce CAPRI's activation logic explicitly (fudge off, bound, widen and activate), or
register "fudge always on" as a departure. The second is defensible: it is smooth and deterministic, and as the
reviewer I consider it better for IPOPT. But it is a departure from CAPRI's runtime behaviour, and the project lead
must decide it.

**Q1.3 (risk) Smooth and non-smooth terms that must be identical.** These include:
- ncpcm in the overfill of TRQImports_ (z = 1e-3·quota; l. 3159-3160);
- the min of bilateral and global tariff in tarSpec_ (z = max(1e-5, 1e-7·|gap|), l. 3326-3334), and in tarAdval_
  with a *different* constant (max(1e-2, 1e-3·|gap|), l. 3448-3449);
- FlexLevyNotCut_ and FlexLevy_ (l. 3246, 3267);
- ProdPriceFudge_ (z = 0.1·min(1e4, max(1e-2, PPRI)), l. 2389-2391).

There are also genuinely non-smooth terms in variables:
- `MIN(0, ·)` and `ABS(·)` in tarSpecIfEntryPrice_ (l. 3212);
- `max(1e-3, v_prodPrice)` in feedBlk_ (l. 989).

These are why CAPRI solves the model as DNLP. Substituting them changes nothing at a solution, but the constants
must be copied exactly. Note: the GAMS comments call these "Fisher-Burmeister" smoothing, and the inventory repeats
this (G3_MARKET_INVENTORY.md l. 13). The function used is ncpcm, the Chen–Mangasarian smoothing
x − z ln(1 + e^{(x−y)/z}). The code's docstring has this right (processing.py l. 224).

**Q1.4 (note) Scaling by base data does not change the solution set.** Division by positive constants such as
`(DATA+0.1)`, `(p_tradeFlows+1)` or `(DATA(arm1P)+1)` changes no solution. In arm1PriceAgg_ and arm2PriceAgg_ the
scaling is inside a degree-1 homogeneous CES and cancels (l. 2952-2956, 3013-3028).

The scaling does change *which approximate point is accepted*. CONOPT's tolerances act on the scaled residuals, and
the "+0.1" or "+1" terms make the effective tolerance absolute for small cells. capri-mod scales its residuals
differently, by max(S0 + D0, 1) (bilateral_solve.py l. 331, 475-478). So two formulations can agree exactly in
exact arithmetic and still accept points that differ at the 1e-5 level. See Q5.

**Q1.5 (gap) Aggregation means G3 is "CAPRI's equations on capri-mod's aggregated data", not CAPRI's model.** A
sum of CES flows over CAPRI's RMs is not a CES in the model region. The same holds for TRQ sigmoids applied to
summed quotas and fills (build_bilateral_market.py l. 236; bilateral_solve.py l. 82-92), and for one Armington ρ
per commodity where CAPRI allows ρ per RM (market_model.gms l. 2715 `p_rhoArm1(RM,XXX)`; build_bilateral_market.py
l. 86-93).

*Recommendation:* the design should state this explicitly. It should drop or qualify "close to CAPRI's GAMS line
for line" (§3.1) where it is read as "the same equilibrium as CAPRI".

**Q1.6 (note) Implicit definitions.** procYield_ is implicit: v_procYield appears on both sides, l. 1517-1519.
The system is linear and its closed form is unique; I verified that processing_ad.py `_cet` (l. 35-43) is that
closed form exactly: S = (A_o + A_c + S0)/2, y_o = A_o + (S0 − S)/2. Elimination is therefore safe here. Similar
care is needed for DairyNQ_/FatsProtBal_ and for the biofuel block (G2f and G2g), which I did not review.

## Q2. The objective and binding bounds

**Q2.1 (error, design gap) CAPRI's behaviour when a bound binds.** m_globMarket is solved as a square DNLP with a
constant objective (FlipFlop_ `v_dummy =E= 10`, l. 3771; SOLVE at simu_market.gms l. 107). If the solution would
lie outside a bound, no feasible point exists. CONOPT then:
1. returns an infeasible point that minimises its (scaled) sum of infeasibilities, with the offending variables at
   their bounds;
2. reports numinfes and suminfes (l. 85-86).

CAPRI then:
1. widens the bounds of the variables that sit at a bound with a nonzero marginal (widen_bounds.gms, called at
   simu_market.gms l. 139-142), or fixes consumption at ~0 (widen_bounds.gms l. 121-123);
2. retries, up to two retries (l. 95);
3. if suminfes > 1e6, raises lower bounds (treat_enormous_infes.gms, l. 113-115);
4. in the last outer iteration, accepts suminfes ≤ 0.1 as "feasible" (l. 289-300).

The bounds are mostly *safeguards*, wide compared with normal solutions:
- quantities 1e-5×–1e3× base (market_model.gms l. 322-323);
- prices 0.1×–10× base (l. 325-326; set_start_val.gms l. 527-528, 563, 574, 733-734, 754-755);
- trade flows ≥ 1e-10 (l. 431).

They are not complementarity conditions of the equilibrium.

**Q2.2 (risk) What IPOPT would return with ½‖z − z₀‖² and the same bounds.** Verified from strings in
`casadi/libipopt.so.3.14.19`:
- "If the problem is square, this option [expect_infeasible_problem] is enabled automatically";
- "Restoration phase found points satisfying feasibility tolerance in square problem";
- "…to recompute multipliers at solution for square problem".

Inference from these and from IPOPT's published algorithm (Wächter & Biegler 2006):
- IPOPT decides "square" structurally: the number of free variables equals the number of equality constraints,
  after fixed variables are removed. A binding bound does not change that count.
- When the root lies outside the bounds, IPOPT enters restoration. There it minimises an l1 constraint violation
  plus a proximity term to the restoration's reference point, and it soon terminates with
  "Infeasible_Problem_Detected" (or a restoration failure).
- The point returned is *not* CONOPT's least-infeasible point (different norm, proximity term and path). It is
  also not an equilibrium. ½‖z − z₀‖² plays no role in it.
- If the problem is square and a root lies inside the bounds, the objective does not change the root. It only
  enters the primal-dual steps and the filter, which may make convergence slower than a pure Newton or feasibility
  iteration.
- If G3 is *not* square: with too many constraints IPOPT stops (too few degrees of freedom). With too few, it
  returns the projection of z₀ onto a solution manifold. That is not CAPRI's equilibrium, and nothing would flag
  it.

**Q2.3 (gap) "z" is undefined in the full-variable form.** In §3.0, z was log r. In §3.1 there are several price
families (v_marketPrice, v_prodPrice, v_consPrice, v_arm1Price, v_arm2Price, v_impPrice, tariffs, v_procMarg,
v_priceFatProt). The design does not say which of them, in which scale (level, ratio, log), with which weights.

**Q2.4 Is the objective defensible?** As a tie-breaker it is harmless but practically inert in the square case.
The design's statement "it only decides which root IPOPT reaches when there are several: the one nearest the
start" is not established. With an isolated root and a nonsingular Jacobian the objective has no effect on the
root. With a singular Jacobian (a flat family, such as the Japanese sunflower cake case in the register,
BEYOND-4), the problem is either non-square in IPOPT's sense (it is not: the count is still square) or it violates
LICQ. Then IPOPT's behaviour is not "nearest root" but unpredictable (unbounded multipliers). What should be
specified:
1. **Squareness before every solve:** the counts and a structural-rank check, as check_square.gms. Abort with the
   unpaired variables listed. This replaces any reliance on the objective.
2. **Objective:** zero, so IPOPT works as a pure feasibility solver as CONOPT does here. Or keep ½‖z − z₀‖², but
   document it as inert in the square case and name z exactly. I consider the zero objective the cleaner and more
   CAPRI-like choice; ½‖z − z₀‖² only becomes meaningful for deliberately non-square formulations, which G3 should
   not have.
3. **Bound policy, explicit.** Pick one and register it:
   - (i) **CAPRI's policy reproduced.** Solve with CAPRI's bounds treated as *checks*, not IPOPT bounds. If a
     variable leaves its bounds, or IPOPT reports infeasibility, apply widen_bounds' rules (widen, activate the
     fudge by setting lo < 0, fix consumption at ~0), then re-solve, at most twice. Report numinfes and suminfes on
     CAPRI's scaled equations with CAPRI's thresholds (1e-5, 0.1).
   - (ii) **An elastic feasibility formulation.** Use c(x) = p − n with p, n ≥ 0, minimise Σ w_i(p_i + n_i) under
     CAPRI's bounds and weights matching CAPRI's scaling. This yields a well-defined least-infeasible point, the
     analogue of CONOPT's phase-1 result. It is a departure in form, not in intent.

   Do *not* pass CAPRI's bounds to IPOPT as hard bounds on a square system without one of these: the result in the
   binding case would be undefined.
4. **IPOPT settings for warm starts** from the previous outer iteration: warm_start_init_point, a small mu_init,
   bound_push/bound_frac. Note bound_relax_factor (default 1e-8): IPOPT relaxes bounds slightly, so a variable can
   end marginally outside CAPRI's bound.

## Q3. Faithfulness of the three built blocks

### G2a: trade with TRQs (trade_ad.py, bilateral.py, bilateral_solve.py)

**Q3.a1 (note, verified) The Armington nests are faithful.**
- importShares_ (l. 2749-2781, with p_arm2Commit = 0, v_gammaCES = 1, pv_theta = 1) is X_ij = M_i β_ij (PM_i/p_ij)^σ2
  (trade_ad.py l. 72, 79; bilateral.py l. 189).
- arm2PriceAgg_ (l. 3013-3028) is PM = (Σ β p^{1−σ2})^{1/(1−σ2)} (trade_ad.py l. 55-56).
- domSalesShares_, arm2QuantShares_ and arm1PriceAgg_ (l. 2706-2730, 2950-2956) are the top nest with the market
  price v_marketPrice as the domestic price (trade_ad.py l. 59-67).
- SupBalM_ (l. 2632-2650) and expQuant_ (l. 3648) appear as S − DS − Σ exports (l. 74-80).
- The calibration (bilateral.py l. 77-85) is consistent: the CES index at base equals the value-weighted price.
  I checked this algebraically: Σ β p^{1−σ} = PM0^{1−σ}.

**Q3.a2 (error, unregistered) Prices of non-supplying regions are held fixed.** z covers only regions with
S0 > 1e-6 (bilateral_solve.py l. 328; trade_ad.py l. 41). The others keep r = rk. CAPRI determines v_marketPrice
of such regions with PMrk_ (l. 3074-3080): Arm1P + PMrkMrg. Where DS = 0 this does not affect the clearing,
because Pd does not enter PA (bilateral.py l. 89). But the reported market price, and any block that reads r for
those regions, stays at base. That includes feed (`noneu_fede(PAr, r, …)`, bilateral_solve.py l. 274). The
inventory has PMrk_ as "to check".

*Recommendation:* in G3, add PMrk_. Until then, register the departure.

**Q3.a3 (gap) Producer price versus market price.** The design and code call r "producer price ratios" (§2;
trade_ad.py l. 9), but in the trade system r scales the *market* price (Pd = Pd0·r, l. 43). CAPRI separates them
with PPri_ (l. 3046-3060): PPri = Pmrk·marg + PSEi + PSEd + AREP(1 − DECP). The two ratios differ wherever the
additive support terms are nonzero.

*Recommendation:* G3 must carry both. For G2b, check that the market supply functions' h (built as
p_hessNQSupp × PPRI0 / INPE per supply.py l. 726-727) are applied to ΔPPri = (PPRI0 − additive support)·(r − 1),
not PPRI0·(r − 1). This is inferred, not verified: the build tool was not reviewed.

**Q3.a4 (error, inventory and register) Global TRQs are absent.** build_trq reads only bilateral quotas: it skips
exporter "World" except for MFN rates (build_bilateral_market.py l. 220-226). Missing:
- TRQImports_ (l. 3142-3162);
- tarSpecW_ and tarAdValW_ (l. 3399-3412, 3475-3487);
- the tarSpec_ branches that take the min of the bilateral and the global tariff (l. 3326-3338) and that apply the
  global tariff to flows without a bilateral quota (l. 3357-3358).

ARMINGTON_MARKET.md l. 145 says this omission is "REGISTERED", but CAPRI_DEPARTURES_REGISTER.md has no row for
it. KNOWN_ISSUES_REGISTRY_LOG.md l. 1137 records CAPRI's EU beef erga-omnes quota as 95% filled at the 2030
reference.

**Q3.a5 (error, unregistered) The ad valorem gap is frozen.** gap = p_out − p_in is computed once at base FOB and
transport cost (build_bilateral_market.py l. 230-231, 236). The offset is gap·(sig − sig0) in EUR/t
(trade_ad.py l. 164). CAPRI's tarAdval_ (l. 3438-3468) makes the *rate* move with the sigmoid, and impPrice_
applies it to the current (Pmrk + transport)·exchange rate (l. 2858-2882). The error is proportional to
(TaMFN − TaPref)/100 · ΔPmrk. It matters for products with large ad valorem MFN rates and large price changes.

*Recommendation:* in G3, split the specific and ad valorem parts and write tarAdval_ in CAPRI's form.

**Q3.a6 (risk, unregistered) TRQs aggregated over exporters and quotas.** Quotas of the CAPRI exporters inside one
model exporter are summed, and one sigmoid acts on the summed fill (build_bilateral_market.py l. 232-236). The fill
is also scaled by `trq_share` = min(1, ImportQ/X0) (bilateral_solve.py l. 88; bilateral.py l. 131). CAPRI has one
sigmoid per (RMTP, RMTP1) pair (l. 3275-3284). Because the sigmoid is steep (slope 200, l. 339: from in-quota to
out-of-quota within about ±3% of the quota), the sigmoid of a sum differs materially from the sum of sigmoids when
fills differ across the aggregated exporters.

**Q3.a7 (gap) Only the EU's quotas are read.** The grep pattern is `^'EU'\.` (build_bilateral_market.py l. 212).
Whether CAPRI's data contain TRQs of other policy blocks I did not check. If they do, it is an unregistered
omission.

**Q3.a8 (note) The tariff is reproduced through an offset relative to the base sigmoid value.** offset =
gap·(sig(fill) − sig(fill0)) (trade_ad.py l. 164; bilateral.py l. 133-137). This equals CAPRI's
TsPref + gap·sig(fill) only if the base import price b already contains TsPref + gap·sig(fill0). Inferred, not
verified: b comes from CAPRI's applied tariffs, which may not equal the sigmoid at base.

*Recommendation:* G3 should write tarSpec_ in levels with CAPRI's parameters (TsPref, TsMFN, TrqNT) and check its
base value against CAPRI's v_tarSpec at the calibration point.

**Q3.a9 (note) The sigmoid argument clip.** The argument is clipped at ±60 (trade_ad.py l. 163; bilateral.py
l. 108, 113-115). CAPRI's `Sigmoid` has no clip. At |arg| = 60 the function is flat to about 1e-26, so the kink is
numerically irrelevant. It can stay, documented.

**Q3.a10 (risk) An import-price floor.** `ca.fmax(pij, 1e-9)` (trade_ad.py l. 52; bilateral.py l. 175, 189) is a
kink not in CAPRI, which instead bounds v_impPrice ≥ max(1e-3 + 1e-5, 0.1·level) (set_start_val.gms l. 518). It is
harmless unless prices collapse, but it should be listed with the other kinks.

**Q3.a11 (gap) G2a is not reusable "as is" in CAPRI's form.** `_system` mixes the Armington block with *local*
log-linear supply and use, S = Sk(r/rk)^es and D = Dk(PA/PAk)^ed (trade_ad.py l. 64-65), and returns only the
residuals and the quota flows (l. 82). The elasticities come from a one-off 1% finite difference, clipped to
[−0.5, 5] and [−5, 0.5] (bilateral_solve.py l. 304-320). That is a path device of the current solver, not CAPRI's
ProdNQ_ (linear in normalised prices, l. 1546-1572) or ArmBal1_. Design §3.1's "reused, not rewritten" therefore
holds only for the CES and TRQ sub-expressions.

*Recommendation:* refactor `_system` into functions that return the flows, PM and PA from the prices, and drop the
local S and D in G3.

### G2c: GL human consumption (demand_ad.py, demand.py)

**Q3.c1 (note, verified) The functional form is CAPRI's.**
- G = √p′B√p, G_i = (B√p)_i/√p_i, F = d′p and x_i = (G_i/G·(Y − F) + d_i)·INHA/1000, with p in EUR/kg
  (demand_ad.py l. 38-42).
- This equals GLDemandFS_, GS_, GiS_ and XiS_ (l. 826-903), including the 1e-3 and 1e-6 price scalings.
- B is symmetrised by the reader (demand.py l. 64-65), as CAPRI fills the lower triangle (market1.gms l. 589).

**Q3.c2 (note, verified) The consumer price is CAPRI's Cpri_ in difference form.** p = CPRI0 + (PR − 1)·ARM1P0 =
CPRI0 + ΔArm1P (demand_ad.py l. 77). This equals Cpri_ (l. 3032-3039) as long as CMrg, CSEd, CSEi (and CTAX for
biofuels) stay at their calibration values. A scenario that changes consumer support or margins is not represented
by this form.

*Recommendation:* in G3, write Cpri_ in levels with the margin terms as parameters.

**Q3.c3 (risk, departure as the code itself says) Two floors.** fmax(p, 0.05·p0) and fmax(x, 0) in the per-cell
sums (demand_ad.py l. 77-78, 89).
- CAPRI's price safeguard is a *bound* at 0.1× the level (set_start_val.gms l. 733), not a floor at 0.05. When it
  binds CAPRI goes infeasible and widens, rather than evaluating demand at a floored price.
- For quantities, CAPRI's runtime rule is to fix consumption at ~0 (Q1.2), not consFudge_.

*Recommendation:* in G3 drop both floors. Treat price-bound and quantity-bound activity with the explicit bound
policy of Q2.4(3), and register the choice. Note that the inventory's "to take CAPRI's [consFudge_] in G3" is
itself a departure from CAPRI's default runtime (Q4).

**Q3.c4 (note) Goods CAPRI fixes respond in capri-mod.** CAPRI fixes consumption where p_pbGL is zero or
HCon ≤ 0.1 (set_start_val.gms l. 176-177; simu_market.gms l. 189); XiS_ is then absent. capri-mod evaluates every
good by the formula. Without B terms that gives d_i·INHA/1000, a constant, which is equivalent if d is calibrated.
Goods with HCon ≤ 0.1 but with B terms respond in capri-mod and not in CAPRI. The effect is small but it is a
domain difference.

**Q3.c5 (note) The level is set by ratios.** H = H0 × GL(p)/GL(p0) per model-region cell (demand.py l. 179-184,
276-279). This transfers CAPRI's relative response to capri-mod's base level. It is consistent with the documented
recalibration (DEP-5), but G3's "defining equation" for consumption is then H − H0·GLR(p) = 0, not XiS_ in levels.
This should be stated.

**Q3.c6 (note) A stale docstring.** demand.py l. 234-236 says the dairy products' processing "stays fixed for now
(audit M9, open)". dairy.py l. 213-221 now implements ProcNQ_ for them with `Harms`. The documentation is
inconsistent.

### G2e: processing and crushing (processing_ad.py, processing.py)

**Q3.e1 (note, verified) These blocks are faithful.**
- ProcMargO_ (l. 1118-1136): seed at Arm1P, oil and cake at producer prices × CET yields (processing_ad.py
  l. 91-96; bilateral_solve.py l. 258-265 sets the seed at the Armington ratio and oil and cake at the producer
  ratio).
- ProcNQ_ for seeds (l. 1140-1152, divided by the fixed DATA PPri INPE; processing.py l. 58-83): q = q0 + H/INPE·Δm,
  with the cross-seed terms.
- procYield_ (l. 1480-1521): the exact closed form (Q1.6).
- ProcO_ (l. 1168-1177).
- ProcNQ_ for other products: −H/INPE·ΔArm1P, where PRCA cancels in the difference (processing_ad.py l. 129-135).

The seed rows of CAPRI's processing Hessian contain no non-seed price (2,338 rows, 0 mixed; my check of
processing_nq_hessian.csv), so restricting the cross terms to seeds (processing_ad.py l. 82) loses nothing.

**Q3.e2 (departure, minor) ProcFudge_ is always active and computed at another level.**
- The fudge is applied always. In CAPRI it applies only when procQuant.lo ≤ 0, i.e. after a widening (Q1.2).
- For the non-seed products it is applied to the *model-region* cell (processing_ad.py l. 139). CAPRI applies it
  per behavioural region.
- z uses the scaled base k·q0 (l. 99) rather than CAPRI's DATA Proc.

At base the correction is e^{−100}-small. Near zero the results differ.

*Recommendation:* register these, or move the fudge to the RMS level in G3.

**Q3.e3 (risk for G3) Hard floors outside the block.** demand.py l. 393 (`max(0, P0 + q − q0)` for crushing) and
l. 482 (`.clip(lower=0)` on all use) are kinks around a smooth block. They are not in the design's kink list (§6,
G2c bullet).

**Q3.e4 (note) Slope versus base when k ≠ 1.** q = k·q0 + H·Δm (processing_ad.py l. 76, 93). The base is rescaled
but the slope is not, so the elasticity at base differs from CAPRI's by 1/k. This is consistent with CAPRI's
practice of keeping H and shifting the constant (e.g. simu_calibration_to_supply.gms l. 356-367 for supply). It
should be noted, not changed.

**Q3.e5 (note) A silent skip.** A Hessian entry (s, y) for which the RMS has no seeds row for y is skipped
silently (processing_ad.py l. 86-88).

*Recommendation:* count and log these.

## Q5. Validation

**Q5.1 (gap) The current acceptance test is not sufficient scientifically.** "Equal to the current solver within
market.tol at every outer iteration, same or fewer iterations" (§6, item 8):
- It compares capri-mod with capri-mod, so it cannot detect any departure from CAPRI. All of Q3's departures would
  pass it: pooled markets, frozen ad valorem gaps, the PMrk_ fix, the floors.
- It conflicts with G3's goal. Every move toward CAPRI's form changes the equilibrium of the current solver and so
  fails the test: consFudge_ or the bound policy, lifting the feed-energy lag (design §8), removing floors, global
  TRQs.
- "Same or fewer outer iterations" is a performance criterion, not a correctness one.
- market.tol = 1e-7 on residuals scaled by max(S0 + D0, 1) (defaults.toml l. 269; bilateral_solve.py l. 475-478)
  is far tighter than CAPRI's own solution accuracy (Q5.3).

*Recommendation:* split G3's acceptance in two.
- **G3-R (regression):** with the current equations unchanged, IPOPT reproduces the current solver's equilibrium.
  This proves the solver change alone.
- **G3-C (CAPRI form):** each CAPRI-form change is validated against CAPRI (Q5.2), one at a time, with its effect
  on the F2F and GD runs reported.

**Q5.2 External validation against CAPRI, concretely.**

*Test A, equation-level replication at CAPRI's own solution. This is the strongest test, independent of the supply
side and the outer loop.*
- **Data:** one CAPRI simulation step's market state after `SOLVE m_globMarket`. That means:
  - all v_* levels (prices, quantities, flows, tariffs, TRQ sigmoids, yields, margins);
  - all parameters in the equations: DATA(…,"CUR"), p_tradeFlows/p_impPrice CUR, p_dpCESTrade, p_rhoArm1/2,
    p_pdGL/p_pbGL, p_cnstNQ*/p_hessNQ* (Supp, Proc, Feed, Dairy), p_trqBilat/p_trqGlobl, p_trqSigmoidSlope,
    pv_prodPriceMarg, PSE/CSE/CMrg, p_exchgRateChangeFactor;
  - the bounds (.lo/.up), the marginals, numinfes/suminfes and the active equation set (equation .l/.m).

  arm/unload_market.gms and arm/unload_market_data_for_debugging.gms are the natural dump points. The project rule
  requires export to CSV. The markmarket dump the project already uses (build_processing_nq.py l. 47) may hold part
  of this.
- **Fixed:** everything exogenous to m_globMarket at that step, including the supply-side constants as CAPRI
  recalibrated them in simu_calibration_to_supply.gms.
- **Compared:** capri-mod's G3 equations evaluated on CAPRI's native sets (RM, RMS, RMTP and products), not on
  capri-mod's aggregation. Evaluate them at CAPRI's levels and report each scaled residual, per equation family.
- **Tolerance:** |scaled residual| ≤ 1e-5 (TOLINFEAS, l. 4388), with a hard ceiling of 5e-4 (rtnwma of conopt.603)
  on equations CONOPT itself reports as satisfied.

This requires G3's equation code to be data-driven on CAPRI's sets. That is a design requirement worth stating now.

*Test B, solve replication.*
- From CAPRI's step-k market state, apply a market-only shock that CAPRI ran: for example the market solve of the
  next step, with the next step's supply constants, or a tariff or TRQ-volume scenario. Solve G3 on CAPRI's native
  sets.
- Compare with CAPRI's solution of that step:
  - prices: Pmrk, PPri, CPri, Arm1P and Arm2P;
  - quantities: prod, hcon, feed, proc and biof per RMS;
  - bilateral flows above 1 kt;
  - TRQ fill and tariffs.
- **Tolerance:** about 1e-4 relative on prices and on quantities above 1% of the region's use, and an absolute
  1e-3 kt below. The comparison must also report CAPRI's own suminfes for the step. Where CAPRI's suminfes > 1e-5,
  compare only the families that CAPRI satisfied.
- Expect differences of order cond(J)·1e-5 from the tolerance alone. Treat larger ones as findings.

*Test C, block tests at stress points (cheap, within capri-mod).*
- TRQ: put each quota's fill in [0.9, 1.05], where the slope-200 sigmoid is steep. At the 2030 base only 3 of 67
  quotas have |200·(fill − 0.97)| < 10 (my computation from bilateral_trq_2030.csv: PARI THA 0.9996, SGMT NOR 0.969,
  SGMT TUR 0.956). Most draws in test_trade_ad.py l. 50-94 therefore sit on the flat parts.
- GL demand: test near price floors and near zero quantities.
- Crushing: test near zero crush.

Also run the tests in the default configuration: the fixtures override `market.market_supply_nq = False`
(test_trade_ad.py l. 11, test_demand_ad.py l. 37, test_processing_ad.py l. 99), while the default is true
(defaults.toml l. 227).

**Q5.3 (note) The tolerance basis.** CAPRI's full solve uses conopt.603:
- rtnwmi 5e-6 and rtnwma 5e-4 (simu_market.gms l. 88; conopt.603);
- m_globMarket.TOLINFEAS = 1e-5 (market_model.gms l. 4388).

Its residuals are relative to base values plus 0.1 or 1. Any comparison with CAPRI below about 1e-5 in scaled
residual measures solver noise.

## Q6. Other issues in the design

**Q6.1 (note) The supply side outside the market with a fixed Hessian is as CAPRI does.** In simulation CAPRI keeps
p_hessNQSupp and re-shifts only the constants each iteration so that the market's supply functions return the
supply models' quantities (simu_calibration_to_supply.gms purpose l. 8-14; l. 356-367). So "supply outside, fixed
Hessian" matches CAPRI. The G3 defining equations must, however, be ProdNQ_ itself:
- linear in normalised producer prices;
- with the PSE/AREP terms for MILK, the CRDO oil-cost term and the feed-bulk-price terms (l. 1546-1572);
- with ProdFudge_ or a bound policy, not the `max(q0 + dq, 0)` of supply.py l. 741 and 825.

**Q6.2 (gap) Switches on by default in CAPRI that capri-mod lacks** (set_global_variables.gms):
- supremaMrk on (l. 386): the market's land supply as a multinomial logit, Yield_ (unconditional),
  LndSupplyEqualsDemand_ and rentFromProdpriceLand_ for the non-RMSSUP regions. capri-mod holds land rents (DEP-7).
- luc_market and lulucf_market, on with REPORTS_ENV (l. 395, 406).
- Data-driven policy blocks: intervention stocks (PADM with STKS), subsidised exports (FEOE_max), flexible levies
  (MinBordP), entry prices (TriggerP), global TRQs, and possibly non-EU TRQs.
- CAPRI's EU_WEST/EU_EAST blocks (DEP-3).

Before G3, the data should be queried for which of these have nonzero parameters at 2030, so that "not ported"
becomes "not ported and inactive in the data" or "not ported and active". The inventory already plans this.

**Q6.3 (risk) Solver robustness.** IPOPT is weaker than CONOPT as a pure equation solver from a good start, and
CAPRI itself needs a heavy apparatus:
- pre-solves by product group with all other prices fixed (simu_prestep.gms l. 205-232);
- grid solves and retries with widened bounds;
- the infeasibility treatment.

Specific risks:
- **The TRQ sigmoid with slope 200.** Its derivative is up to 50 per unit of fill; CAPRI's comment l. 335-337 notes
  "potentially produces more infeasibilities".
- **CES exponents.** ρ2 = 10 by default, up to 13.4 for pork per KNOWN_ISSUES l. 1137. With tiny flows (lower bound
  1e-10, set_start_val.gms l. 431) this gives badly conditioned Jacobians.
- **The kinks of Q3.**
- **An indefinite Lagrangian Hessian.** This forces inertia corrections. In the square case it only matters
  through the barrier and the objective, so a zero objective also makes the exact Hessian largely irrelevant. With
  a zero objective and no inequality constraints, the Hessian of the Lagrangian is Σλ_i∇²c_i, and its effect on
  the steps is limited. This is inferred.

*Recommendations:*
1. Keep the current fixed-point solver as an automatic fallback.
2. Optionally warm-start G3 from one fixed-point pass.
3. Specify the IPOPT options: tol and constr_viol_tol on CAPRI-scaled residuals; nlp_scaling_method;
   warm-start settings; the linear solver.

**Q6.4 (gap) Variable scaling.** Prices range from 1 to 10⁴ EUR/t and flows from 1e-10 to 1e5 kt. CAPRI relies on
equation scaling, with Scaleopt = 0 (l. 4384), and leaves variables in levels. For IPOPT I recommend variables as
ratios to base, x/x0 (or logs for prices), where x0 > 0, and an absolute scale where x0 = 0. This is a numerical
choice, not a model change. As the reviewer I consider it better than CAPRI's level variables for an
interior-point method, and it should be registered as such.

**Q6.5 (risk) Size and duplication.** The design accepts tens of thousands of unknowns (§3.1), but capri-mod's
aggregation has far fewer regions than CAPRI. Since Q5.2 recommends that G3 run on CAPRI's native sets for
validation, the design should state which set structure G3 targets in production (capri-mod's aggregation) and in
validation (CAPRI's sets), and keep one equation code for both.

**Q6.6 (note) The feed-energy lag.** The design is right that the lag (`_bilateral_eval` l. 267-274) affects only
the path at convergence. Lifting FeedBal_ in G2d is correct. The same applies to the dairy price of the previous
evaluation used by Norway (DEP-29).

**Q6.7 (gap) Departures to add to the register** (the register says it "must be complete"):
- pooled commodities with a world balance;
- PMrk_ (prices of non-supplying regions held);
- global TRQs (stated in ARMINGTON_MARKET.md l. 145 but absent from the register);
- TRQ quotas and fills aggregated per model exporter, and the share-scaled fill;
- the ad valorem gap frozen at base FOB;
- EU-only TRQs;
- ProcFudge_ always on and at model-region level;
- the GL floors at 0.05·p0 and x ≥ 0;
- the kinks of Q3 (demand.py l. 298, 393, 482; supply.py l. 741, 825; trade_ad.py l. 52);
- one Armington ρ per commodity instead of per RM.

## Beyond CAPRI (for the project lead's decision)

- **A deterministic least-infeasibility mode** (Q2.4 (ii)). It would give a reproducible, documented answer where
  CAPRI's retry-and-widen sequence is path-dependent. It is a departure in mechanics, not in model.
- **Structural diagnostics before each solve:** a Dulmage–Mendelsohn decomposition of the G3 Jacobian. CAPRI only
  counts equations and variables (check_square.gms l. 22). This would report the unpaired equations and variables
  and any structurally singular block, such as the Japanese sunflower cake family.
